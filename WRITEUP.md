# Satellite Tile Enhancement: Technical Report

## 1. Headline Answers and Pipeline Rationale
* **Does SR beat bicubic?** **Yes.** Pretrained FSRCNN 2× outperforms bicubic upscaling across all variants: **+1.21 dB PSNR** (95% CI: [+1.03, +1.40] dB) on clean tiles and **+0.68 dB PSNR** (95% CI: [+0.58, +0.78] dB, SSIM +0.018) under realistic blur, noise, and JPEG degradation (100% win rate).
* **Does classical help or hurt?** **On full-reference fidelity, it hurts clean and noisy imagery, but rescues heavy haze.** On clean tiles, classical processing collapses PSNR to 16.3 dB ($\Delta\text{SSIM} = -0.39$); on blur/noise, it drops PSNR by **-11.27 dB** (95% CI: [-12.81, -9.77] dB), driven almost entirely by contrast expansion. It only improves PSNR under synthetic haze (+3.42 dB) and combined degradation (+3.73 dB).
* **Pipeline Ordering:** Operations run strictly from low to high frequency: Non-Local Means color denoising ($h=3$) executes first to suppress high frequencies before contrast stretching; CIELAB percentile clipping (1st–99th) and CLAHE (`clipLimit=2.0`, $8\times8$ grid) expand local luminance without chromatic shift; unsharp masking ($\sigma=1.0$, threshold 3.0) sharpens structural boundaries last. White balance is omitted to avoid neutralizing natural vegetation and soil tones.

## 2. Evaluation Design, Biases, and Reference Strategy
Ground truth was established by applying synthetic downsampling, Gaussian blur, sensor noise, and Koschmieder optical haze ($t=0.7, A=220$) to native tiles ($N=41$). We considered querying Google Static Maps with `scale=2` to obtain genuine optical $2\times$ pixel pairs without synthetic degradation. However, requiring live API calls introduces external billing, rate limits, and mandatory API keys that prevent offline, self-contained reproduction. Synthetic degradation is a weaker proxy for physical lens MTF and non-uniform atmospheric scattering, but guarantees fully reproducible baselines.
* **Metric Biases:** Full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM) heavily penalize contrast stretching: expanding dynamic range increases $L_2$ pixel distance even when visual interpretability improves. Bicubic downsampling artificially favors FSRCNN (+1.86 dB) because it matches the model's training kernel. Crucially, Laplacian variance as a sharpness metric is flawed: it rewards high-frequency noise amplification over true edge definition.

## 3. Three Failure Cases on Google Tiles
Stage ablations identify three distinct failure modes on Google aerial imagery ([inspection crops linked](file:///home/harshil-byggari/Documents/Perse_Assignment/Satellite-tile-enhancement/failures/)):
1. **Dynamic Range Distortion & Color Drift** ([`failures/case1_contrast_aba1eb.png`](file:///home/harshil-byggari/Documents/Perse_Assignment/Satellite-tile-enhancement/failures/case1_contrast_aba1eb.png)): On `building_aba1eb_google`, color drift reached $\Delta E = 55.82$. Stage ablation confirms this is driven entirely by `contrast_only`, which collapsed PSNR by **-14.54 dB** (from 25.77 dB down to 11.23 dB, while `denoise_only` and `sharpen_only` preserved $\sim 26$ dB). Percentile clipping saturated dense rooftop highlights and skewed chroma.
2. **Sensor Noise Amplification & Laplacian Bias** ([`failures/case2_noise_e42da1.png`](file:///home/harshil-byggari/Documents/Perse_Assignment/Satellite-tile-enhancement/failures/case2_noise_e42da1.png)): On `building_e42da1_google`, wavelet noise $\sigma$ spiked from 4.79 to 14.19 (+9.40), while Laplacian variance artificially jumped $7.6\times$ (3,822 to 29,121). Stage ablation shows lifting underexposed asphalt shadow floors exposed sensor grain, which unsharp masking amplified into visible noise that Laplacian variance falsely rewarded as sharpness.
3. **JPEG Blockiness Seam Amplification** ([`failures/case3_blockiness_cac04a.png`](file:///home/harshil-byggari/Documents/Perse_Assignment/Satellite-tile-enhancement/failures/case3_blockiness_cac04a.png)): On `building_cac04a_google`, the 8×8 blockiness ratio surged from 0.704 to 0.808 (+0.104). Stage ablation reveals `denoise_only` reduced blockiness (0.677), but `contrast_only` widened inter-block boundary steps (0.778) and unsharp masking sharpened them (0.808), turning faint DCT compression seams into prominent grid lines across smooth pavement.

## 4. 50,000 Tiles/Day Throughput & Real Bottlenecks
Single-core CPU benchmarks on 640×640 equivalent tiles: Classical is 0.54s/tile (~160k tiles/day/core); FSRCNN is 0.30s/tile (~290k tiles/day/core); Bicubic is 0.003s/tile.
At 50,000 tiles/day (~7.5 core-hours), CPU compute is not what breaks first. Primary production bottlenecks are:
1. **Google API Quota & Cost:** Ingesting 50k tiles/day (1.5M/month) triggers severe rate limits and commercial query fees.
2. **Storage Restrictions:** Persisting raw or processed tiles in an application database raises immediate ToS compliance barriers.
3. **I/O & Memory Under Concurrency:** Network fetching and uncompressed image buffers consume RAM rapidly under concurrent batch workers.
4. **Algorithmic Scaling:** NLM denoising consumes >90% of classical CPU time; on high-resolution tiles ($>1000\times1000$), latency scales quadratically (estimated ~8.5s/tile). Production pipelines should replace NLM with fast guided filtering (estimated <15ms) and export FSRCNN to ONNX Runtime (estimated <65ms).

## 5. Google Maps Platform Terms of Service
Storing enhanced satellite tiles in a persistent database to serve an internal product appears to conflict with the Google Maps Platform Terms of Service. Under Section 3.2.3 ("License Restrictions"), Section 3.2.3(a) and 3.2.3(b) prohibit pre-fetching, caching content beyond temporary performance windows (capped at 30 days), and creating independent databases of Google content. Section 3.2.3(a) further bars modifying, adapting, or creating derivative works of imagery, which super-resolution and contrast enhancement constitute. Section 3.2.3(c) requires content to be displayed exclusively within Google Maps Core Services (Maps SDK / JavaScript API), precluding custom Leaflet or MapLibre servers, while Section 3.2.2(b) mandates that Google logos and copyright watermarks remain visible and unmodified.
**Do Not:** (1) Store tiles in a permanent database; (2) strip Google watermarks or copyright notices; (3) display tiles outside authorized Google Maps SDKs; (4) feed tiles into machine learning without explicit permission. Always obtain formal legal review before deployment.
