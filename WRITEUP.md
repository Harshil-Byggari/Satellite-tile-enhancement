# Satellite Tile Enhancement: Technical Report

## 1. Headline Answers and Pipeline Rationale
* **Does SR beat bicubic?** **Yes.** FSRCNN 2x consistently outperforms bicubic: **+0.68 dB PSNR** (95% CI: [+0.58, +0.78], SSIM +0.018) under realistic blur/noise/JPEG (100% win rate). On Google tiles, the gain is **+0.57 dB** (+0.97 dB clean). The bicubic_ds advantage (+1.53 dB) reflects training-kernel bias (~0.32 dB above clean).
* **Does classical help or hurt?** **Classical globally hurts real tiles and degraded baselines.** On blur/noise, it drops PSNR by **-11.27 dB** (95% CI: [-12.81, -9.77]; SSIM -0.17). It only improves PSNR under haze (+3.42 dB), at the cost of SSIM (-0.15). On real tiles, it raises noise 2.8x (sigma: 0.60 to 1.70), sharpness 6.2x, and mean Delta E to 27.9 ('radioactive' saturation). Contrast expansion is responsible (contrast_only costs -10.26 dB); denoise_only is the sole stage that helps (+0.88 dB, SSIM +0.069).
* **Pipeline:** Low-to-high frequency: NLM color denoising (h=3), CIELAB percentile clipping (1st-99th) and CLAHE (clipLimit=2.0, 8x8 grid), and unsharp masking (sigma=1.0, threshold 3.0). White balance is omitted. Key flaw: an 8x8 CLAHE grid on small tiles (75x103) yields tiny ~10px cells that over-amplify local contrast.

## 2. Evaluation Design, Biases, and Reference Strategy
Ground truth was synthesized via downsampling, blur, noise (sigma=3.0), JPEG (Q=60, 65, 70), and Koschmieder optical haze (t=0.7, A=220) on native tiles (N=41). We considered querying Google Static Maps scale=2 for optical 2x pairs. We bypassed this because live API calls require billing, quotas, and keys that prevent offline reproduction; furthermore, scale=2 often returns bilinearly interpolated server pixels rather than genuine sensor MTF.
* **Biases & Disclosures:** Mapbox tiles inflate aggregates (clean PSNR ~40 dB, Laplacian ~168, indicating pre-smoothed data); Google-only metrics give the true baseline. Three tile pairs (b31756, c85563, f73de6) share identical coordinates. Full-reference metrics penalize contrast stretching. Sobel correlation is vacuous for haze (reads 0.9996) because haze is an affine transform. On clean tiles, classical absolute PSNR is 17.9 dB (capping delta artifact -82 dB), proving clean-tile distortion. Laplacian variance is biased: it rewards noise amplification over true sharpness.

## 3. Three Failure Cases on Google Tiles
Stage ablations isolate three failure modes on Google aerial tiles ([crops in failures/](failures/)):
1. **Contrast Blowout & Color Drift** ([case 1 crop](failures/case1_contrast_aba1eb.png)): On building_aba1eb_google, color drift reached Delta E = 55.82. Stage ablation confirms this is driven entirely by contrast_only, which collapsed PSNR by **-14.54 dB** (from 25.77 to 11.23 dB, while denoise_only and sharpen_only preserved ~26 dB). The 1st-99th percentile stretch and CLAHE over-saturated bright roofs and skewed chroma.
2. **Noise Amplification & Laplacian Bias** ([case 2 crop](failures/case2_noise_e42da1.png)): On building_e42da1_google, wavelet noise sigma surged from 4.79 to 14.19 (+9.40), while Laplacian variance exploded 7.6x (3,822 to 29,121). Stage ablation confirms lifting underexposed shadow floors exposed sensor grain, which unsharp masking amplified into visible noise that Laplacian variance falsely rewarded as sharpness.
3. **Learned SR Structural / SSIM Failure** ([case 3 crop](failures/case3_sr_ssim_20d961.png)): On building_20d961_google, FSRCNN 2x performed **worse than bicubic** on structural similarity: SSIM dropped from 0.577 to 0.556 on bicubic_ds (delta SSIM = -0.021) and from 0.576 to 0.572 on clean (delta SSIM = -0.004). On dense repeating rooflines and parking lots, FSRCNN's shallow 5-layer convolutions introduce phase smearing and structural misalignment compared to smooth bicubic interpolation.

## 4. 50,000 Tiles/Day Throughput & Real Bottlenecks
Single-core CPU benchmarks on 640x640 tiles: Classical is 0.88s/tile (NLM consumes 0.85s or 95.5% of latency); FSRCNN is 0.60s/tile; Bicubic is 0.004s/tile (smaller mixed dataset tiles averaged 0.30s classical, 0.20s FSRCNN).
At 50k tiles/day (~12.2 core-hours for 640x640), CPU compute is not what breaks first. Primary bottlenecks are:
1. **Google API Quota & Cost:** Ingesting 50k tiles/day (1.5M/mo) triggers severe rate limits and query fees.
2. **Storage Compliance:** Persisting tiles in a database conflicts with provider caching terms.
3. **I/O & Memory Under Concurrency:** Network fetching and uncompressed buffers saturate RAM under concurrent workers.
4. **Algorithmic Scaling:** NLM denoising consumes >95% of classical CPU time; on high-resolution tiles (>1000x1000), latency scales quadratically (estimated ~8.5s/tile). Fast guided filtering (estimated <15ms) and ONNX Runtime CPU inference (estimated <65ms) should replace them.

## 5. Google Maps Platform Terms of Service
Storing enhanced satellite tiles in a persistent database to serve a product appears to conflict with Google Maps Platform Terms of Service. Under Section 3.2.3 ('License Restrictions'), Section 3.2.3(a) and (b) prohibit pre-fetching, caching content beyond temporary performance windows (capped at 30 days under 3.2.3(a)), and creating independent databases of Google content. Section 3.2.3(a) also bars modifying, adapting, or creating derivative works of imagery. Section 3.2.3(c) requires displaying content exclusively within Google Maps Core Services (Maps SDK / JS API), precluding custom Leaflet or MapLibre servers, while Section 3.2.2(b) mandates that Google logos and copyright watermarks remain visible and unmodified.
**Do Not:** (1) Permanently store tiles in a database; (2) strip Google watermarks or copyright notices; (3) display tiles outside authorized Google Maps SDKs; (4) feed tiles into machine learning without explicit permission. Always obtain formal legal review before deployment.
